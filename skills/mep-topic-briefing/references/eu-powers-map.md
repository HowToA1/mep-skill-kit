# EU Powers Map: Who Can Do What (for any MEP committee topic)

*MEP Skill Kit v1.0 · by Dimitar Bidzhekov*

The fastest way to win a General Assembly is to ask the question most delegates can't answer:
**"Under which article, and by whom?"** The fastest way to lose is to write a clause the EU
has no power to deliver. Use this map on every topic. Items marked *(verify)* should be
re-checked before quoting. This is debate preparation, not legal advice.

## 1. Competences by topic

| Competence type | Treaty basis | Policy areas | What a resolution can realistically ask |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Exclusive** (only the EU legislates) | Art. 3 TFEU | Customs union; competition rules for the internal market; monetary policy for the euro area; conservation of marine biological resources; common commercial (trade) policy; some international agreements | Direct EU action: "Calls on the Commission to propose / negotiate..." |
| **Shared** (EU and member states; the EU acts first where it chooses) | Art. 4 TFEU | Internal market; parts of social policy; cohesion; agriculture and fisheries; **environment**; consumer protection; transport; trans-European networks; **energy**; area of freedom, security and justice (**migration, asylum, police and judicial cooperation**); common safety concerns in **public health**; research, technology, space; development and humanitarian aid | EU legislation is possible: directives, regulations, targets, frameworks |
| **Coordination** | Art. 5 TFEU | Economic, employment and social policy coordination (European Semester) | Benchmarks, country-specific recommendations, common targets |
| **Supporting** (the EU only supports and coordinates) | Art. 6 TFEU | **Human health**; industry; **culture**; tourism; **education, youth, sport**, vocational training; civil protection; administrative cooperation | Funding (Erasmus+, EU4Health), exchange of best practice, recommendations. **No harmonising national laws.** |
| **Foreign and security policy (CFSP/CSDP)** | Art. 24 TEU; Arts. 42–46 TEU | Foreign policy, sanctions, **defence** | Mostly unanimity in Council; Parliament is consulted and informed. Ask the Council or High Representative, or point to industrial and market tools (EDF, joint procurement) |
| **Digital and AI** | Mostly Art. 114 TFEU (internal market) | AI Act, DSA, DMA, GDPR (Art. 16 TFEU) | Strong EU power through single-market rules |

Housing, healthcare systems, education systems, national taxation, wages and the core of
policing remain largely **national**. Remember that when the topic sounds "European".

## 2. Institutions and powers

| Institution | Power that matters in a resolution |
|---|---|
| **European Commission** | **Right of initiative** (Art. 17 TEU): proposes laws; enforces EU law (infringements); manages the budget; negotiates trade deals |
| **European Parliament** (720 MEPs in 2024–2029; **17 from Bulgaria**) | Co-legislator; co-decides the budget; consents to international agreements and enlargement; can **request** the Commission to propose a law (Art. 225 TFEU); scrutiny, inquiry committees, motion of censure |
| **Council of the EU** (ministers) | Co-legislator; unanimity in sensitive areas; adopts CFSP decisions |
| **European Council** (heads of state or government) | Sets direction and priorities; **does not legislate** |
| **Court of Justice of the EU** | Interprets EU law; annuls acts; infringement rulings |
| **European Central Bank** | Euro monetary policy; independent (Bulgaria is now in the euro area) |
| **Agencies** | Frontex, EMA, ECDC, ENISA, EEA, Europol, AI Office, etc. Name the right one in a clause |
| **EIB** | Lending and guarantees; a useful funding actor for almost any topic |

## 3. How EU law is made
- **Ordinary legislative procedure** (Art. 294 TFEU): Commission proposal → Parliament and
  Council amend and adopt as equals → informal "trilogues" → adoption. Most laws follow it.
- **Special legislative procedures:** Council decides, Parliament is consulted or must give consent (for example tax, some social security).
- **Citizens' initiative:** 1 million signatures from at least 7 member states can invite the Commission to propose a law.
- **National parliaments:** subsidiarity check under Protocol No 2, the "yellow card".

## 4. Types of EU act (Art. 288 TFEU)
| Act | Binding? | Use in a clause |
|---|---|---|
| Regulation | Binding, directly applicable everywhere | "Calls on the Commission to propose a regulation..." when uniform rules are needed |
| Directive | Binding on the goal; states choose the means | When national systems differ |
| Decision | Binding on its addressees | Specific cases |
| Recommendation / opinion | Not binding | Supporting-competence areas; tax; education |
| Communication, strategy, action plan | Political, not legal | "Calls on the Commission to present a strategy..." |

## 5. Unanimity zones (one member state can veto)
Taxation (Arts. 113 and 115 TFEU); CFSP (most decisions); **enlargement** (Art. 49 TEU);
the Multiannual Financial Framework (Art. 312 TFEU) and own resources (Art. 311 TFEU); parts
of social security and social protection; family law with cross-border effects; Art. 7 TEU
(rule of law, at the determination stage); Treaty changes.
→ A clause in these areas should *call on the Council* or *member states* and expect
compromise. A strong move is to **propose a shift to qualified majority voting** using the
passerelle clause (Art. 48(7) TEU), and to know that this itself needs unanimity.

## 6. Money
- The current budget period runs **2021–2027**; the **2028–2034 Multiannual Financial
  Framework** is being negotiated now (the Commission proposed it in July 2025 *(verify the
  total and the structure)*).
- **NextGenerationEU / Recovery and Resilience Facility** money must be committed by 2026 *(verify the deadlines)*.
- Common funding hooks: cohesion funds (ERDF, ESF+, Cohesion Fund), Horizon Europe, Erasmus+,
  EU4Health, CEF, Digital Europe, the European Defence Fund, the Social Climate Fund, the
  Innovation Fund, the EIB, and the Just Transition Fund (important for Bulgarian coal regions).
- **Every new spending clause should name a source**: reallocation, the next MFF, the EIB,
  national co-financing, or a new own resource (which needs unanimity).

## 7. Subsidiarity and proportionality test (Art. 5 TEU), 30 seconds per clause
1. Who acts, and is it the right level?
2. Can member states achieve this well alone? If yes, the EU coordinates, funds or recommends.
3. Is there a cross-border element (single market, pollution, migration flows, security, platforms)?
4. Is this the least restrictive measure that works?
5. Who pays, and when will we know it worked?

## 8. Who to address in an operative clause
| Actor | Verbs | Good for |
|---|---|---|
| European Commission | Calls on, Requests, Invites, Urges | Proposals, strategies, guidance, funding, enforcement |
| Council of the EU / member states | Calls on, Encourages, Recommends | National measures; unanimity areas |
| European Council | Calls on, Invites | Political priorities, enlargement decisions, MFF |
| High Representative / EEAS | Calls on, Urges | Foreign policy, diplomacy |
| An EU agency (name it) | Requests, Invites | Technical work, data, monitoring |
| EIB | Invites, Calls on | Loans, guarantees, investment platforms |
| Regional and local authorities | Encourages, Invites | Local implementation |
| The (Model) European Parliament itself | Commits, Resolves, Decides | Oversight, follow-up, its own committees |
| Companies, platforms, civil society | Calls on, Encourages | Codes of conduct, voluntary commitments |

## 9. Red-flag phrases and safer rewrites
| Red flag | Why it gets attacked | Safer version |
|---|---|---|
| "The EU bans / orders / forces..." | Parliament can't order; the Commission has initiative | "Calls on the Commission to propose..." |
| "Introduces an EU-wide tax on..." | Tax needs unanimity; no general EU tax power | "Recommends that member states consider...; invites the Commission to study..." |
| "Harmonises national education / health systems" | Supporting competence only (Art. 6 TFEU) | "Encourages exchange of best practice and funding through Erasmus+ / EU4Health" |
| "Creates an EU army" | CFSP/CSDP is intergovernmental; unanimity | "Calls for deeper defence cooperation through PESCO, the European Defence Fund and joint procurement" |
| "Guarantees every citizen X" | Unfunded and unmeasurable | "Sets a voluntary EU target of X by 20XX, monitored through the European Semester" |
| "Creates a €100 billion fund" | No named source; budget is fixed to 2027 | "Calls for a dedicated envelope in the 2028–2034 MFF and, meanwhile, the reallocation of unused funds" |
| "Bans citizens of country X from..." | Discrimination; free movement; Charter | Rules based on conduct, use or risk, not nationality |
| "Expels member state X" | No expulsion mechanism exists | "Calls on the Council to proceed under Article 7 TEU" |
