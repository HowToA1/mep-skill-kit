# Worked Examples: What "Excellent" Looks Like

*MEP Skill Kit v1.0 · by Dimitar Bidzhekov*

### 2. The European Affordable Housing Plan, 16 Dec 2025 (VERIFIED)

- **Four pillars:** (I) boosting supply; (II) mobilising investment; (III) enabling immediate support while driving reforms; (IV) supporting the most affected. Within them, **ten key areas of action**. [Council doc ST-17011-2025]
- **Money:** more than **€43 billion** mobilised by the EU for housing in the 2021–2027 budget; **€10 billion** more to be mobilised from the EU budget in 2026–2027; **€375 billion** to be mobilised by partner financial institutions by 2029. [commission.europa.eu]
- **State aid:** the revised SGEI Decision recognises **affordable housing** (not only social housing) as a category exempt from notification, without a maximum compensation threshold. [Loyens & Loeff analysis of the revised Decision]
- **Pan-European Investment Platform** for affordable and sustainable housing, built with the EIB Group and national promotional banks. [EIB / Commission]
- **European Housing Alliance** of member states, cities, regions, EU institutions, housing providers and civil society; and a first EU Housing Summit in 2026. [Commission / Euronews]
- Promises the Affordable Housing Act (see section 4) and promotes digital planning and permitting.
- Respects subsidiarity: housing remains first and foremost a competence of member states, regions and cities. [Commission reply to EP resolution]

### 3. The European Parliament's housing report, 10 Mar 2026 (VERIFIED)

Adopted 367–166–84. Non-binding. Main asks, as reported by the EP and press:
- **Supply and red tape:** a simplification package, digital permitting, and a **60-day deadline** for granting permits.
- **Tax:** incentive-based tax measures for low- and middle-income households; **lower registration fees for first-time buyers**; tax conditions that make long-term renting more affordable.
- **Short-term rentals:** the upcoming law should balance tourism with access to affordable housing, with common EU objectives and implementation left to member states, regions and cities.
- **Tenants and owners:** condemns squatting and wants stronger protection for owners; also wants stronger tenant rights and limits on disproportionate rent increases.
- **Social housing:** an adequate share of public and social housing in EU cities.
- **State aid:** any revision should make public investment in social housing easier.
- **Funding:** better coordination of EU funds; **reallocate unused Recovery and Resilience Facility money** to social, public, cooperative and affordable housing.
- **Renovation and quality:** earmark funds for renovation and energy poverty; quality standards for new homes.
- **Industry and skills:** "Made in EU" origin requirements for components in EU co-financed projects; training, fair wages, labour mobility, recognition of qualifications.
- Rapporteur Giménez Larraz: Europe is **short 10 million homes** and **rents are up more than 30%**. His line: *"A generation that cannot afford a home cannot build a future."*
- Political reading: critics on the left described the vote as a "grand coalition" for a market-led answer (Rosa Luxemburg Stiftung); property-owner groups (UIPI) welcomed it as balanced. Use this to show both sides.

### 4. The Affordable Housing Act proposal, 9 Sep 2026 (VERIFIED)

COM(2026) 599, 2026/0268(COD). Status: **proposal**, going through the ordinary legislative procedure.
- Creates a **common methodology** for authorities to identify **"areas under housing stress"**, based on public data.
- Gives a common framework for measures affecting the use of housing: **short-term rentals**, **vacant dwellings**, **second homes** and other non-primary uses.
- Measures must be **"targeted, necessary and proportionate"** and must be shown to contribute to local housing pressure.
- **Short-term-rental restrictions:** authorities must show significant adverse effects on affordability or availability for **at least three years**, and that **less restrictive measures would not be equally effective**. Measures must target activity that removes long-term housing stock (scale, frequency, commercial character).
- Conditions linked to **acquisition** cannot apply retroactively; transitional arrangements are required.
- Long-term vacancy measures should allow for justified periods of non-occupation.
- The Commission says the Act does not create new EU-law rights to restrict STRs. It frames when and how authorities can act. Housing policy stays national.
- Accompanied by a **Commission Recommendation** on housing affordability and supply in stressed areas: faster planning and permits, renovation, repurposing buildings, **Housing Acceleration Plans**.
- Eurobarometer, cited by the Commission: **more than 50% of city dwellers** see housing as an immediate, urgent problem where they live.
- Reactions: Renew Europe welcomed the legal certainty for cities; critics (reported by Skift) say the rules lack oversight.
- BACKGROUND: a leaked draft (reported by Euractiv) excluded short-term rental of **primary residences** from the framework. Check whether the final text kept this.

### 5. Short-term rentals: rules already in force (VERIFIED)

- **Regulation (EU) 2024/1028** applies since **20 May 2026**: hosts register, platforms must check registration numbers and remove non-compliant listings, and data flows to public authorities.
- The Affordable Housing Act would sit on top of it and tell authorities when restrictions are justified.

### 6. Headline data (VERIFIED unless marked)

**Prices and rents (Eurostat, release of 1 Oct 2026)**
- Q2 2026 vs Q2 2025: EU house prices **+4.7%**, rents **+3.0%**. Euro area prices **+4.0%**.
- Q2 2026 vs Q1 2026: prices **+1.2%**, rents **+0.7%**.
- Annual EU price growth is slowing: Q3 2025 +5.5%, Q4 2025 +5.4%, Q1 2026 +5.1%, Q2 2026 +4.7%.
- Fastest annual rises in Q2 2026: **Portugal** (first), **Bulgaria +15.5%** (second). See section 10 for Portugal's exact figure.
- Long run (Eurostat, Oct 2025): between 2010 and Q2 2025, EU house prices **+60.5%** and rents **+28.8%**. Hungary +277%, Estonia +250%, Portugal +141%. Italy was the only country where prices fell.

**Young people**
- Average age of leaving the parental home: **26.3 in 2025** (26.2 in 2024). Highest: Croatia 31.5; Greece and Slovakia 30.9; Spain and Italy 30.2. Lowest: Finland 21.4, Denmark 21.8, Estonia and Lithuania 22.7. [Eurostat, Sep 2026]
- **49.8%** of EU residents aged 18–34 lived with at least one parent, or shared in the parental household's income, in 2025. [Eurostat via Xinhua, Sep 2026]
- **Housing cost overburden** (housing costs of 40% or more of disposable income): **9.1%** of young people aged 15–29 vs **7.7%** of the whole population in 2025. 2024: 9.7% vs 8.2%. In 2024 the highest youth rates were Greece (30.3%) and Denmark (28.9%). [Eurostat]
- **Overcrowding:** 26.9% of 15–29-year-olds lived in overcrowded homes in 2025, against 16.8% of the whole population. Romania was highest at 60.4%. [Eurostat, Oct 2026]
- Youth employment rate (20–29): 65.5% in 2025. Countries where young people leave home earlier mostly have higher youth employment. [Eurostat]

**Other**
- Homelessness: about **1.3 million** people homeless in the EU in 2025 (FEANTSA estimate cited by the EU Fundamental Rights Agency, Jun 2026).
- New housing supply met only about **50% of demand** by late 2025 (EIB 2025, cited by Eurofound).
- Home-ownership rate: 68.5% EU (2025). Households spent on average 19% of disposable income on housing (2024). [Eurostat via Cyprus Mail]

### 7. Host country: Portugal and Porto

VERIFIED
- Portugal had the EU's **sharpest annual house-price rise** in Q2 2026 (Eurostat). Prices more than doubled between 2010 and 2025 (+141%).
- **IMT Jovem** (since 1 Aug 2024): buyers aged 35 or under pay no property transfer tax (IMT) and no stamp duty on a first home that will be their own permanent residence. In 2026, full exemption up to **€330,539**, partial above that. **More than 70,000** young people benefited by January 2026.
- **State guarantee** (Decree-Law 44/2024): the state guarantees up to **15%** of the purchase value of a mortgage for buyers aged 35 or under, making up to 100% financing possible. Around **23,000** buyers had used it by January 2026, with contracts open until the end of 2026.
- The debate point: these are **demand-side** subsidies. Press coverage notes prices kept rising as they rolled out. A classic argument is that subsidies get "capitalised" into prices when supply cannot respond. Use it as a case study, not a verdict.
- Tax: from 2026 tenants can deduct up to €900 of rent from income tax.
- **Porto:** the City Council cancelled about **1,413** Alojamento Local (short-term rental) licences in early 2026 for breaching regulations. Porto uses AL "containment" areas.
- **Porto metro area:** sale prices and rents rose by more than 50% in most districts since 2022, while household incomes grew by only 18% (Century 21 Portugal study, Feb 2026).
- **Porto social housing:** 12,409 municipal dwellings, 28,180 residents, average rent about €77 (Porto Municipal Social Housing Observatory, Jan 2026).
- **Porto new-contract rents:** about +30% in 2022, +12% in 2023, +2% in 2024, −1.6% in 2025.
- Average age of leaving home, Portugal: 28.9 (2024 data). Check the 2025 figure.

BACKGROUND (verify before quoting)
- Portugal ended the real-estate route of its Golden Visa in 2023 (Mais Habitação package).

### 8. Bulgaria quick sheet

VERIFIED
- Joined the euro on **1 January 2026** (21st euro-area member).
- House prices **+15.5%** year on year in Q2 2026, second in the EU; **+4.5%** quarter on quarter (Eurostat via BTA).
- House prices rose **125%** between Q1 2010 and Q1 2025 (Eurostat via Sofia Globe).
- Average age of leaving the parental home: **28.2 (2024)**, down from 30.0 in 2023 (Eurostat via BTA). Check the 2025 figure.
- Mortgage rates around 2.5–3.5%. Reported: cutting banks' minimum reserve requirement from 12% to 1% after euro adoption freed roughly €8 billion of liquidity (Novinite, Jan 2026). Construction costs up 15–18% in 2025 (industry data).

### 9. Speculation: what is happening and what is contested

VERIFIED
- **Spain:** in January 2025 PM Pedro Sánchez proposed a tax of up to **100%** on home purchases by **non-resident, non-EU** buyers, and later floated an outright ban. Law firms (Baker McKenzie, Cuatrecasas) flag serious doubts about compatibility with EU **free movement of capital (Art. 63 TFEU)**. Critics say non-EU buyers are only about 3–5% of transactions. **Check the current status before citing.** Spain ended its golden visa from 3 April 2025.
- **Catalonia** has pursued fiscal measures explicitly aimed at curbing speculation (Baker McKenzie).
- **Short-term rentals** are the speculation-adjacent issue the EU is actually legislating on (sections 4–5).

BACKGROUND (verify before quoting)
- **CJEU, Cali Apartments (C-724/18 and C-727/18, 22 Sep 2020):** a Paris-style authorisation scheme for repeatedly renting furnished homes short-term is justified by an overriding public-interest reason, namely fighting the shortage of long-term rental housing.
- **CJEU, Libert (C-197/11 and C-203/11, 2013):** Flemish conditions requiring a "sufficient connection" to the municipality before buying land restricted EU freedoms. Social-housing aims can justify restrictions, but that scheme went beyond what was necessary.
- **CJEU, Konle (C-302/97, 1999):** prior authorisation for buying land as a second home in Tyrol was disproportionate where a declaration system would do.
- **Denmark** keeps a Treaty protocol letting it restrict second-home purchases by non-residents. Malta has a similar arrangement for secondary residences.
- **Netherlands:** since 2022 municipalities can bar buyers of cheaper homes in designated areas from renting them out ("opkoopbescherming", buy-to-let protection).
- **Berlin's rent cap** (Mietendeckel) was struck down by Germany's Federal Constitutional Court in 2021 because rent law is federal. This is a lesson about competence, not only economics.
- **Barcelona** announced it will not renew roughly 10,000 tourist-apartment licences when they expire in November 2028.
- **Vienna:** roughly half of residents live in municipal or limited-profit subsidised housing, the classic social-housing model.
- **Ireland** introduced a Vacant Homes Tax; **France** taxes vacant homes in tight markets.

### 10. Known data conflicts (check before quoting)

- **Portugal's Q2 2026 annual house-price rise:** BTA reports **17.8%**, Euronews reports **16.5%**, both citing Eurostat. Both agree Portugal was first and Bulgaria second (15.5%). Check Eurostat `prc_hpi_q` and quote the official figure, or say "around 17%, the highest in the EU".
- **Portugal's youth mortgage-guarantee envelope:** reported as €1.55 billion (after a €350 million top-up) and elsewhere as €2.3 billion. Quote the number of beneficiaries (about 23,000) rather than the envelope unless re-checked.
- **"Rents up more than 30%"** (rapporteur) and **"rents +28.8% since 2010"** (Eurostat) describe different periods and bases. Say which one you mean.

## Example 2 — Worked Example Resolution (housing), for learning the pattern

This example shows format and reasoning on one real EU topic. It is **not** for bringing
into committee as a pre-written text. Use it to see how introductory clauses set up
operative clauses, how each operative clause names an actor and a lever, and how a
resolution balances ambition with legal reality. Facts were verified on 17 Nov 2026.

---

Committee on the Housing Crisis (HOUS) submits the following resolution to the Model
European Parliament about regulating housing speculation while ensuring affordable housing
for young people.

The Model European Parliament,

A. *Alarmed by* the 4.7% annual rise in EU house prices and the 3.0% rise in rents in the
second quarter of 2026, with Portugal and Bulgaria recording the steepest increases in the
Union (Eurostat, 1 October 2026),

B. *Noting with deep concern* that young Europeans leave the parental home at an average age
of 26.3, and that 9.1% of people aged 15 to 29 live in households spending at least 40% of
their disposable income on housing, compared with 7.7% of the population as a whole
(Eurostat, 2025 data),

C. *Concerned* that new housing supply met only around half of demand by late 2025 (European
Investment Bank), while homes continue to be withdrawn from long-term residential use
through commercial short-term letting, prolonged vacancy and speculative investment,

D. *Recalling* the European Affordable Housing Plan of 16 December 2025 and the European
Parliament resolution of 10 March 2026 on the housing crisis in the European Union,

E. *Welcoming* the Commission's proposal of 9 September 2026 for an Affordable Housing Act,
which establishes a common methodology for identifying areas under housing stress,

F. *Bearing in mind* that housing remains primarily a competence of member states, regions
and cities, and that measures affecting the acquisition of property must respect the free
movement of capital and the right to property,

G. *Aware* that support for young buyers through transfer-tax exemptions and state mortgage
guarantees can push prices up where supply cannot respond,

1. **Calls on** the European Parliament and the Council to adopt the Affordable Housing Act
without delay, and supports amendments ensuring that:
   a) member states may apply non-discriminatory owner-occupancy or long-term-rental
   requirements to existing homes purchased below a regional price threshold in areas under
   housing stress,
   b) restrictions on short-term rentals target entire homes let on a commercial basis while
   protecting the occasional letting of primary residences,
   c) the Commission evaluates the Act's effect on rents and on youth affordability three
   years after it starts to apply;

2. **Recommends** that member states consider taxes on long-term vacant dwellings, with
exemptions for justified non-occupation, and capital-gains taxation on non-primary
residences that decreases with the length of ownership, while recognising that taxation
remains a national competence;

3. **Urges** member states to direct the revenue from such measures into affordable rental
housing for people under 35;

4. **Requests** that the Commission and Eurostat develop, by 2028, comparable indicators on:
   a) vacant dwellings,
   b) the share of residential purchases made by companies and multi-property investors,
   c) rent-to-income ratios for people aged 18 to 34;

5. **Invites** the European Investment Bank to open, within the Pan-European Investment
Platform for affordable and sustainable housing, a dedicated financing window for
cooperative, limited-profit and public rental housing that reserves a share of units for
students and young workers;

6. **Encourages** member states to make full use of the revised State aid rules for services
of general economic interest to fund affordable housing for young people without prior
notification;

7. **Calls for** unused Recovery and Resilience Facility funds to be reallocated to social,
public, cooperative and affordable housing, and for a dedicated affordable-housing envelope
in the 2028–2034 Multiannual Financial Framework;

8. **Recommends** that member states offering tax exemptions or mortgage guarantees to young
first-time buyers pair them with measurable commitments to increase housing supply in the
same areas, and publish annual evaluations of their effect on prices;

9. **Encourages** regional and local authorities to adopt Housing Acceleration Plans with
digital permitting, working towards the 60-day permit deadline proposed by the European
Parliament, and to make public land available on long-term lease for affordable housing;

10. **Proposes** a European Youth Housing Guarantee under which member states, supported by
EU funds, aim to offer young applicants affordable rental housing or housing support within
a defined period, with progress reported annually through the European Semester.

### Clause-defence table (extract)
| OC | Likely attack | 1-line defence |
|---|---|---|
| 1a | "Violates free movement of capital" | "It applies to every buyer equally, only in stressed areas and only below a price threshold. It's about use, not nationality." |
| 2 | "The EU can't tax" | "That's why we *recommend*. Tax stays national." |
| 7 | "Where's the money?" | "Named sources: unused RRF funds now, a dedicated MFF envelope from 2028." |
| 8 | "You're against helping young buyers" | "We keep the help and add the supply, so it doesn't vanish into higher prices." |

### Why this works
- Every IC sets up an OC: prices (A) → 1–3; youth burden (B) → 3, 5, 8, 10; supply (C) → 5, 7, 9; law (F) → the careful wording of 1a and 2.
- Contested measures are framed by **use**, not **nationality**, so they survive the legal attack.
- Money has named sources, so "who pays?" already has an answer.
