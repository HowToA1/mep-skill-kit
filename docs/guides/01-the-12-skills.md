# The 12 Skills: What, When, How and Which Model

*MEP Skill Kit v1.0 · by Dimitar Bidzhekov*

**MEP Skill Kit v1.0 · Delegation of Bulgaria · 64th iMEP Porto**

Each skill below has: **what it does**, **when to use it**, **how to ask for it** (copy these example requests), **which model** to pick, and **what you get back**.

You never need to type the skill's name. Asking naturally is enough.

---

## 1. Topic Briefing

**What it does:** turns any committee topic into a complete study pack: what the EU is allowed to do on this topic, which laws and proposals already exist, the key statistics (with dates and sources), what's happened in the last three months, the main arguments on each side, real examples from different countries, Bulgaria's angle, and the ten questions your committee will argue about.

**When:** the moment you receive your committee topic.

**How to ask:**
- "We got our topic: [paste the committee name and question]. Crash mode first, please." (one page, for when time is short)
- "Now give me the full study pack."
- "Go deep: positions of all the political groups and a practice question set."

**Best model:** Opus 5.5 · **Alternative:** Sonnet 5.5 · **Settings:** web search on.

**You get:** a structured study pack you can read in about 10 minutes, with sources.

## 2. Bulgaria Position

**What it does:** builds Bulgaria's position paper on your topic: Bulgaria's numbers, what Bulgaria has already done, the government's stance on each option (clearly marked as *officially stated*, *based on Bulgaria's laws and votes*, or *our reasoned guess*), three things to fight for, red lines, likely allies and opponents, and the hard questions Bulgaria will face, with 30-second answers. It also writes the head-of-delegation introduction speech.

**When:** right after the topic briefing, and before the opening ceremony for the introduction.

**How to ask:**
- "What is Bulgaria's position on [topic]? Who are our allies and what will they attack us on?"
- "Write our head-of-delegation introduction, 2 minutes."

**Best model:** Opus 5.5 · **Alternative:** Sonnet 5.5 · **Settings:** web search on.

**Why it's special:** it never invents what the Bulgarian government thinks. If no official position exists, it says so.

## 3. Resolution

**What it does:** helps you write the committee's resolution in the correct MEP format: introductory clauses lettered A, B, C and operative clauses numbered 1, 2, 3, with the right phrases and punctuation. Every action it suggests names *who* does it, *with what power or money*, *by when*, and *how we'll know it worked*.
- **Before the session:** it gives you a bank of clause **ideas** with stronger and softer versions, because many sessions don't allow you to bring a finished resolution.
- **During committee work:** it turns what your committee agreed into a clean, correct resolution, plus a "defence table" explaining how to defend each clause.

**How to ask:**
- "Give me clause ideas for [topic], grouped by theme."
- "Here are our committee's notes: [paste]. Turn them into a proper MEP resolution with a defence table."

**Best model:** Opus 5.5 with extended thinking (Fable 5.2 if you have it) · **Alternative:** Sonnet 5.5.

## 4. Red Team

**What it does:** acts as the toughest delegate in the room. It scores a resolution out of 40 on eight criteria (legality, specificity, funding, evidence, youth relevance, coherence, chance of passing, originality), lists the five most dangerous weaknesses with ready-made fixes, checks every fact, and predicts the "speech against" word for word, with your best replies.
- **Defend mode:** for your own resolution.
- **Attack mode:** for other committees' resolutions during lobbying. It finds openings and suggests questions and amendments.

**How to ask:**
- "Red-team our resolution like the toughest delegate in the room: [paste]."
- "Attack mode: find the weak spots in the ENVI committee's resolution: [paste]."

**Best model:** Fable 5.2 or Opus 5.5, with extended thinking · **Alternative:** Opus 5.5 · **Settings:** web search on.

## 5. Amendments

**What it does:** writes amendments in the correct format (change, add or delete text in an operative clause), with a one-line reason, a 60–90 second defence speech, likely questions and answers, and a prediction of who will vote for it. For your own committee, it writes the 2-minute response recommending for or against someone else's amendment. Its fast mode gives just the essentials during the General Assembly.

**How to ask:**
- "Write an amendment to clause 5 of the ENVI resolution that fixes the missing funding: [paste the clause]."
- "Someone proposed this amendment to our resolution: [paste]. Should we accept it? Write our response."

**Best model:** Sonnet 5.5 · **Alternative:** Haiku 5.5 (during the session).

## 6. Speeches

**What it does:** writes speeches timed to the second: the head-of-delegation introduction, the opening speech presenting a resolution, the speech against, the closing speech (with gaps to fill in live when you answer the speech against), amendment speeches, and 30-second answers. Each comes with a word count, the estimated time, marks showing where to pause and what to stress, backup lines in case you're challenged, and what to cut if time runs short.

**How to ask:**
- "Write a 3-minute opening speech for our resolution. Main message: [one sentence]."
- "Shorten this speech to 2 minutes: [paste]."

**Best model:** Sonnet 5.5 to write, Opus 5.5 for the final polish · **Alternative:** Haiku 5.5.

## 7. Sparring

**What it does:** your practice partner. It plays eight types of tough delegate (the Lawyer, the Treasurer, the Sceptic of Brussels, the Market Delegate, the Rights Delegate, the Youth Voice, the Neighbour, the Data Hawk) and asks you **one question at a time**. After each answer it gives you a score out of 10, what worked, the one thing to fix, and a model answer. Other modes: it gives a full speech against your resolution for you to answer, ten rapid-fire questions, or a committee negotiation role-play.

**How to ask:**
- "Grill me on our resolution, difficulty 3. One question at a time."
- "Give a speech against our resolution, then let me answer."

**Best model:** Sonnet 5.5 · **Alternative:** Haiku 5.5 for rapid fire · **Tip:** use **voice mode** and answer out loud, just like in the plenary.

## 8. Live Copilot

**What it does:** gives you something to say within seconds, on your phone: a reply of 45 words or fewer in correct parliamentary style, one fact to back it up, an "if they push back" line, a sharp question to ask back, or the right procedural move ("raise your placard and say...").

**When:** during breaks, lobbying and committee work, and in the plenary only if devices are allowed.

**How to ask:**
- "Quick: the delegate from France says our plan has no legal basis. What do I answer?"
- "They just quoted a number on migration. Is it right?"
- "What point do I raise if I can't hear the speaker?"

**Best model:** Haiku 5.5 (fastest) · **Alternative:** Sonnet 5.5 · **Tip:** use the Claude app on your phone.

## 9. Fact-Check

**What it does:** checks every number, date, law and quote: yours before you speak, and other delegates' when they quote something. Each claim gets a verdict (✅ correct, 🕒 correct but outdated, ⚠️ misleading, ❌ wrong, ❓ can't be verified), the correct version with date and source, and a safe way to say it. For someone else's claim, it suggests a polite challenge.

**How to ask:**
- "Fact-check every number in this speech before I deliver it: [paste]."
- "Is it true that [claim]?"

**Best model:** Opus 5.5 · **Alternative:** Sonnet 5.5 · **Settings:** web search must be on.

## 10. Lobbying

**What it does:** helps you win people over. It maps what each delegation really wants (often different from what it says), suggests compromise wording that both sides can sign, merges competing clauses, designs "you support this, we support that" deals, counts the likely votes delegation by delegation, and writes short persuasive messages to other delegations. It does all of this diplomatically and honestly.

**How to ask:**
- "Germany and Italy disagree on clause 3. Find wording both can accept."
- "Count our votes: who's for, who's against, who can we win over?"

**Best model:** Opus 5.5 · **Alternative:** Sonnet 5.5.

## 11. Session Tracker

**What it does:** keeps a clean record of the whole session as it happens. You type quick notes like *"AFET resolution passed 98-41-12, we voted for"* or *"French amendment to clause 4 failed"*, and it organises everything. At any time it can show you the status, statistics (what passed, by how much, who votes like Bulgaria) and a timeline. At the end, everything feeds into the debrief.

**How to ask:**
- "Start tracking the session: 64th iMEP Porto, we are Bulgaria."
- "Log: ITRE resolution failed 60-75-10, we voted against."
- "Show the stats so far." · "Who votes with us?"

**Best model:** Sonnet 5.5 · **Alternative:** Haiku 5.5 for quick logging · **Settings:** "Code execution" on (for the statistics).

**Why it matters:** this record is the first version of the MEP session-tracking platform being built for Bulgarian and international MEP delegations.

## 12. Debrief

**What it does:** turns experience into skill. Each evening it runs a 5-minute check-in (five short questions) and gives you a plan for tomorrow. After the session it writes a full report (what we proposed, how votes went, which arguments won, what to do differently), a handover note for the next Bulgarian delegation, honest LinkedIn or Instagram posts if you want them, and feedback on the kit so version 1.1 is even better.

**How to ask:**
- "Debrief today with me, then plan tomorrow."
- "Write our final session report."
- "Write a LinkedIn post about the session. Factual, no exaggeration."

**Best model:** Sonnet 5.5 · **Alternative:** Opus 5.5 for the final report.
